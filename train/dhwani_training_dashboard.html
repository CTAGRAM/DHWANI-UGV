1 · Where it trained

1024 rovers were simulated in parallel on a Tesla T4 (Kaggle) GPU, each an Ackermann JetRacer — a chassis on a free joint, two position-controlled steering knuckles and four wheels — driving across one procedurally rough heightfield. The engine is NVIDIA's Newton 1.6.0 (Warp 1.17.0), the MuJoCo solver handling every wheel-on-ground contact.

The whole point of a GPU sim is that per-step cost barely moves as rovers are added: 1024 rovers run at 72k environment-steps every second, so 109 hours of driving experience take 10.1 minutes of wall time.

5 · What actually ships to the rover

ON THE NANO · no GPU, no internet

The trained policy exports to a plain .npz of weights. On the Jetson — which has no CUDA and no TensorRT — it runs as a numpy MLP.

Verified on the robot at 49 Hz. It publishes to a gated topic, never straight to the wheels — a sim-trained policy meeting real terrain is switched on deliberately, not by starting a node.
